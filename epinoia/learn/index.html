What Epinoia is

One data pipeline, from the scorer's table to every screen

Epinoia is a basketball competition and analytics platform. It captures every game, live from a statistician's laptop, phone or tablet, or from a league's existing feed. It turns each game into a play-by-play log and derives from that one log everything a league, a club, a coach, a broadcaster and a fan could want: the table, the box score, the advanced numbers, the scouting reports, the alerts and the data feeds.

The result is the depth of statistics that professional leagues pay specialist providers for, made available to every level of the game, in English, Japanese and Spanish, on the web and on the phone. It is already live across the leagues counted above, many of them read from the feeds they already publish, so a competition can be covered before it has signed anything.

The opportunity

Most basketball is played without the numbers the top tier enjoys

The gap

Depth stops at the top tier

Below the elite leagues, statistics are a box score at best and a photograph of a scoresheet at worst.

Lineups, on/off, shot zones, play types and scouting reports are bought by a handful of professional clubs. Everyone else does without, though their coaches, players and supporters ask the same questions.

The waste

The data exists, scattered

Many leagues already publish play-by-play somewhere, in a format nobody else can use.

Epinoia reads those feeds as they are, in over twenty source formats, and turns them into one standard record, so the same analysis runs on a Japanese, a Spanish and an English league alike, and players can be compared across them.

The audience

Fans, players and recruiters want it

A league that publishes real statistics is a league people follow, report on and scout.

Players need evidence for the next contract, recruiters need to compare leagues, and supporters and sponsors follow competitions that feel professional. Live scores, alerts and profiles are what make that happen.

The timing

Phones, not equipment

Capture needs a phone and a browser. Analysis runs in the cloud. Delivery is the web, apps and email.

What once took a broadcast truck and a data contract now costs a volunteer statistician and a web page, which makes the long tail of basketball reachable for the first time.

Why Epinoia

What makes it hard to copy, and easy to adopt

Coverage without contracts

Adapters for more than twenty feed formats read leagues' existing statistics, so a competition is covered, and its players scouted, before it signs anything; a signed league then adds live scoring and its own site.

Pro-level analysis at every level

BPM, adjusted plus-minus, lineups, situation splits and a win model are usually sold to the top tier. Here they are computed for every league with play-by-play, automatically, from the first game.

One engine, no disagreement

The scorer, the site and the server run the same code over the same log, so a correction in April moves every number from September at once, and a table can be rebuilt from scratch with the same answer.

Built for the real hall

Local-first scoring survives no signal; a phone reads the scoreboard clock; volunteers learn it in one game in training mode.

Global from day one

The site reads in English, Japanese and Spanish with each country's own basketball vocabulary, names are transliterated consistently, and every league keeps its own colours.

Safe and private by design

Minors are withheld at the database, readers' personalisation stays on their device, analytics count visits without identifying anyone, and data-rights requests run on a recorded clock.

The platform, page by page

Captures of the running site

How the numbers are made

From an event log to every statistic, the same way everywhere

Everything is an event: a made two, a defensive rebound, a substitution. Each is a row appended to a log that is never edited in place: an append-only event log. One shared engine computes the box score, the lineups, the possessions and every advanced figure from that log, and it is the same file in the scorer, on the site and on the server.

Possessions
Counted from the play-by-play, chance by chance, so pace, ratings and per-100 figures are measured rather than estimated wherever the log exists.
Lineups
Every stint of every five, with its own box score at both ends, from the substitutions; the base of on/off, WOWY, rotations and the starters and bench splits.
Situations
Every chance labelled: second chance, transition, off a turnover, after a timeout, half court; assisted or not, and from which zone.
Shot zones
At the rim, mid-range and three, with volume, accuracy and the share that was assisted, for players, clubs and leagues.
Corrections
A correction appends a retraction; the log is replayed and every figure moves together. Standings, awards and brackets are recomputed from the log, never accumulated.

The models

Estimates of value and of what wins, refreshed as games finish

Learns from every game

What wins

Every finished game is reduced on the server to a line of 108 counts per side, and the models refit from the new lines within the hour.

Per league and season it explains what decided the games played, forecasts from the season so far, and simulates a game possession by possession; a pooled model across every league's season gives the smaller leagues sound priors. Rebuilt from nothing every week.

Player value

BPM 2.0, season and single game

Box plus/minus for every player's season, and Basketball-Reference's game-level adaptation for every game.

A game's BPM uses the player's season position and role, and the team rating from who actually played, calibrated to the competition's own level; it ranks the weekly stars.

Impact

RAPM and on/off

Regularised adjusted plus-minus straight from the event log, and on/off and with-or-without splits from every stint, so a player is credited for what happens while he plays, adjusted for who he played with.

Context

Game percentiles

Each game number is read against a season of real games, replayed through the same engine, so a line shows as good or poor without knowing the league.

Significance

What a game is worth

Cup finals, top-of-the-table meetings and record nights are scored, lifting their match reports in readers' feeds and explaining why.

Schedule

Strength of schedule

Each club's results set against the quality of the opponents it has faced.

Shooting

Expected shooting and consistency

The return a side's shot diet should yield against what it made, and how steady a shooter is from three.

Availability

Injuries, inferred

Who is missing, worked out from the games: a regular absent from the last ones is flagged, without anyone typing a list.

Integrity, privacy and safeguarding

Enforced by the database, not by remembering

Safeguarding
Players recorded as minors are withheld from public pages, the API and every partner feed unless consent is recorded, by a row-level policy on the players table, so every reader inherits it.
Access
Who may write is decided by the database: a finished game cannot be reopened by a reader, a statistician cannot touch another competition.
Readers' privacy
Personalisation is learnt and kept on the reader's device; visit analytics count pages without identifying anyone and honour Do Not Track.
Data rights
Access, correction, erasure and complaints go to a recorded queue with its legal clock.
Security
Row-level security on the platform's tables, secrets only on the server, signed links for members' files.
