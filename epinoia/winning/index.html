How it is worked out

Definitions, sample sizes and what the numbers cannot say

Three lenses, never mixed

Explains
What decided the games already played. Each game’s differences between the two sides are set against its margin. This is accounting: it says where the points came from, not what to change.
Forecasts
What the numbers so far say about the next games. Season-to-date profiles, checked week by week against games the model had not seen, and shown only where they beat a simple rating.
Model
What if a team changed one thing. A possession-by-possession simulator calibrated on the league. It is a model’s answer, never proof of cause.

The measures

Every finished game is reduced once to a line of counts per side. The four factors are effective shooting (eFG%), turnovers per play (TOV%), offensive rebounds won (OREB%) and getting to the line: the free-throw attempt rate, free-throw attempts per 100 field-goal attempts (FTA/FGA). Whether the free throws go in is FT%, a measure of its own. Their competitive versions leave out garbage time: a side 20 or more ahead with under eight minutes left, or 13 ahead with under four.

A difference model has no intercept. Home court is a term of its own, worth a few points beyond the factors. One team-SD is how far apart a good and an average side usually are on a measure. Points become wins through the spread of results around a pre-game expectation, never through the much smaller spread the factors leave over.

How sure we can be

Every estimate carries a 95% range from a bootstrap that resamples whole weeks of games. A measure whose q-value is 0.05 or more is drawn grey: not distinguishable from noise. Small leagues lean on every league’s pooled answer, and the page says how far.

Minimum samples

  • A league’s own model: 20 finished games (below 200 it is shown shrunk toward the pool)
  • A bin on a curve: 30 games; a hard number: 50 games
  • A team-season: 8 games; a forecast test game: 3 earlier games for each side, and 60 test games before a forecast is shown
  • A lineup cell: faint under 200 possessions; squad evidence: low power under 50 team-seasons

What the numbers cannot say

  • Accounting is identity, not levers.
  • Leaders slow down, and trailing sides foul and shoot threes. Competitive time and the pace of the first three quarters reduce this, not remove it.
  • Talent confounds style and squad shape.
  • The simulator moves one rate with the others fixed.
  • Positions are estimated.
  • Leagues differ in level and gender; carrying an answer across them is an assumption.
  • A q-value is not causal evidence.
