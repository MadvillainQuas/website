EPINOIΛ privacy

Your data

What you can ask Epinoia to do with the personal data it holds about you, how long each takes, and the form that reaches the person responsible for data protection. Asking costs nothing.

01

Make a request or a complaint

What do you want to do?

To delete your Epinoia account, send this with Erase it chosen. We delete the account and what it holds: your email address, your name, what you follow, your notification subscriptions, and your EPINOIA GO stamps and photographs. If a record has to be kept, we tell you which and why; statistics stay, without your name. Signed in, the request comes from the account; signed out, give the address it uses.

How long it takes

A request
We answer within one month of receiving it. If we need proof of who you are, the month starts when we have it.
The clock stops
If we have to ask what you mean or which data you want, the days until you reply do not count towards the month.
Complex requests
A complex request, or several at once, can take up to two further months. If it will, we tell you within the first month, and why.
A complaint
We acknowledge a complaint within 30 days, aim to resolve it within three months, and tell you what we did.
Cost
Nothing. We can refuse a request that is plainly unfounded or excessive, or charge a reasonable fee for it; if we do, we say why and how you can challenge it.
For a child
A parent or guardian can ask on a child’s behalf. We may check that you are, and an older child may be asked too, because the data is theirs.
04

What each right means

See your data
A copy of the personal data we hold about you, with what we use it for, who we share it with, how long we keep it and where it came from.
Correct it
Inaccurate data corrected and incomplete data completed. A result or a box score is a record of what happened: we can correct your name on it, not change the game.
Erase it
Your data deleted when we no longer need it, when you withdraw a consent we relied on, or when you object and we have no overriding reason to keep it. Some records have to be kept for a time, such as safeguarding or discipline records, and we tell you which and why. Statistics stay, without your name. Erased data can remain in our encrypted backups until they expire, where nobody uses it.
Restrict it
We keep your data but stop using it, for example while we check whether it is accurate or consider an objection.
Object
To a use of your data that relies on our legitimate interests, such as a public player profile. We stop unless we have compelling reasons to continue. If it is marketing, we always stop.
Take it elsewhere
The data you gave us, where we use it with your consent or to provide a service to you, in a common format a computer can read.
Complain
About anything to do with how your personal data has been handled here. You can also complain to the regulator (below).
Notifications without an account
A notification button on a league’s website keeps your browser’s push address and what you chose to follow, and nothing about you: no name, no email. Turning notifications off, on the button or in its window, deletes it at once, and so does a push address your browser has given up. The notifications sent to it are deleted after seven days.
05

Who is responsible

Epinoia is responsible (the “data controller”) for your account and for what this site does with your data. Where a governing body or an independent league has registered you through Epinoia, that organisation is responsible for its registrations, and you can choose it in the form above.

The form above reaches the person responsible for data protection at Epinoia.

If you are unhappy with how we handle a request or a complaint, you can complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office, the UK regulator, at ico.org.uk or on 0303 123 1113. They usually ask you to raise it with us first.

Epinoia on Android

What the app is
The Epinoia app for Android shows this website, full screen, in Chrome. It is the same site with the same account, so everything on this page applies to it. The app itself adds a notification channel for game alerts and a screen for its notification settings, and keeps nothing about you of its own.
Your account
Your email address; your name, if you give it or sign in with Google; the clubs and players you follow and what you want to be told about; and, if you turn notifications on, your phone’s notification subscription (its push address and keys, which browser or app it belongs to, and whether the latest notification was delivered) and the notifications sent to it.
Players, including children
Clubs and leagues enter their players’ records: names, shirt numbers, teams, birth years (including for players under 18, who are marked as minors) and a photograph once it is approved. You can ask to see, correct or erase such a record with the form above, as a parent or guardian if it is about your child.
Email
Emails we send you, such as notifications you choose to have by email, go through Resend, which delivers them for us and uses your address for nothing else.
Ads and tracking
None. There are no adverts, no advertising trackers and no one else’s analytics, and nothing about you is sold or shared for marketing. Like the website, the app counts which pages are opened, anonymously: see Visit counts below.
Notifications
Optional. The app asks once; you can say no, and turn them on or off at any time in the app’s notification settings or in Android’s settings for the app.
Location
Only for EPINOIA GO: when you press its button to find the game you are at or to stamp an arena, or open its list of today’s games or its find-a-game page to see how far each one is. Android asks first; what happens to your location is below.
Delete your account
Choose Erase it in the form above and send it; this link chooses it for you.

EPINOIΛ on iPhone

What the app is
The EPINOIΛ app for iPhone shows this website, full screen. It is the same site with the same account, so everything on this page applies to it. The app itself adds notifications through Apple and keeps nothing about you of its own beyond your sign-in, which stays on the phone.
Your account
The same as on the website: your email address; your name, if you give it; the clubs and players you follow and what you want to be told about; and, if you turn notifications on, your iPhone’s notification address from Apple (a device token, which says nothing about you or your phone beyond where to send a notification), that it belongs to the iPhone app, whether the latest notification was delivered, and the notifications sent to it.
Apple
Notifications to the app are delivered by Apple Push Notification service, which receives each notification’s title, text and link in order to show it on your phone. Apple’s own handling of that is covered by Apple’s privacy policy.
Ads and tracking
None. The app does not track you across other apps or websites, shows no adverts, and contains no one else’s analytics. Like the website, it counts which pages are opened, anonymously and linked to no one: see Visit counts below.
Camera
Only if you choose to take a photo to upload, or to read a scoreboard clock. iOS asks first, and nothing is recorded unless you start it.
Location
Only for EPINOIA GO: when you press its button to find the game you are at or to stamp an arena, or open its list of today’s games or its find-a-game page to see how far each one is. iOS asks first; what happens to your location is below.
Notifications
Optional. iOS asks the first time you turn them on; you can turn them off at any time on your Profile page or in the iPhone’s Settings, then Notifications, then EPINOIΛ.
Delete your account
In the app, open your Profile page and tap Delete my account, or choose Erase it in the form above and send it; this link chooses it for you.
08

EPINOIA GO

Your location
Used only when you press a button on EPINOIA GO, or open its list of today’s games or its find-a-game page to see how far each one is. Finding the games near you, and how far they are, is worked out on your phone, from a list of games: your location is not sent for it. Passport mode never uses it: the place you pick stays on your screen for that visit. When you stamp an arena, your location is sent once, to check you are there, and is not stored. The map on the find-a-game page is Google’s and loads only when you open a game: Google is asked for the arena, not for where you are.
What a stamp keeps
The arena, the game, its league, the time, how precise your phone said its location was, and your note about the game if you write one. Never where you were. Only you can see your notes. Your stamps are private too, unless you go public (below), and you can take a stamp back.
Your tries
Each try at a stamp is noted: the game, whether it worked and why not, never your location. It stops one account trying without end, and the notes are forgotten after 30 days.
Going public
Only if you choose, only if you are 18 or over, and with a username: one tap turns it on and another turns it off. It shows your username and your numbers (arenas stamped, games stamped and the distance between them) on the leaderboards, and your stamps on the feed: the game, the arena and the date, under your username. Never your email or your notes. If you joined the leaderboards before the feed showed stamps, your stamps stay private until you say otherwise.
Your photographs
Only of games you stamped. Before a photograph leaves your phone it is resized, which removes its hidden details, including where it was taken. A person looks at every photograph before it is public; once it is, it shows your username, the game and the arena. You can remove it at any time. No photographs are accepted from youth leagues’ games.
Deleting your account
Takes your stamps, your choices and your photographs with it, and the photographs’ files are removed.
09

Visit counts

What is counted
Which pages of the site are opened and which tabs on them are chosen, so we can see what is read and make it better: the page, the league, club or game already named in its address, and the name of a tab.
What each count carries
Four facts about the visit that describe no one: whether you are signed in (yes or no, never who), whether it is a phone, a tablet or a computer, the language the site is shown in, and whether it is the app or a browser. If you came from another website, that website’s name, never the page.
What is never kept
Your account, your email address, your IP address, your browser’s details, a cookie, or anything that lasts beyond the browser tab. The counts are anonymous for good: nothing in them can be tied back to you, now or later, because nothing that could identify you is ever recorded.
How one visit is told apart
By a random code your browser makes for that tab and keeps in its session storage, which the browser deletes when the tab is closed. The code is linked to nothing, and your next visit gets a new one.
What you search for
When you use the search box, the words you typed are kept (in lower case, without accents or punctuation) with how many results it showed, whether you picked one and which league, club or player, and the same three facts as a visit. They are kept with no code at all, not even the visit’s, so a search cannot be tied to you, to a visit or to another search. Anything that looks like an email address, a phone number or a web address is never kept.
Who sees them
Only Epinoia’s platform administrators, as totals, such as visits a day or the most read leagues. They go straight to Epinoia’s own database: no other company’s analytics or advertising service is involved, and nothing is shared or sold.
How long they are kept
400 days, then they are deleted.
Turning it off
If your browser sends Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track, nothing is counted. You can also switch it off here, for this browser.
